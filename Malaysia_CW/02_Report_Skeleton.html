Report skeleton (Tasks 1-5)

Format: 12-pt Times New Roman, 1.5 line spacing, tables and figures in the body, references at the end. Total: 7 pages plus references. Rough word guide at this spacing: about 350-400 words per page of text, less if the page has a diagram or table. Each author writes their own text.

Task 1: Country profile and pandemic scale (Islom) - 1 page, 20 marks

  • Country snapshot (2-3 lines, with a source)
  • Pandemic table: cases, deaths, excess mortality, stringency index (peak and dates), economic impact. At least two international sources
  • Main government support measures (name, size, date, % of GDP)
  • Table of goods and services whose price suddenly rose or fell: market, shock type, % change, period, source
  • Why they matter for social well-being and the economy (1-2 sentences each)

Marking reminder: every claim about the size of a change needs a cited figure.

Task 2: Demand shocks (Otabek, O'lmasbek) - 2 pages, 25 marks (about 1 page per market)

  • Diagram: demand and supply, three dated periods, axes with actual P and Q values
  • Explanation of the change using the collected data
  • Case study: one named firm, sector body or public institution, with a dated source
  • Stabilisation actions by companies, the government and foreign countries, and what the data show about their effect

Task 3: Supply shocks (Bagdad, Ulug'bek) - 2 pages, 25 marks (about 1 page per market)

Same structure as Task 2.

Task 4: Elasticities (Islom writes; everyone calculates their own) - 1 page, 20 marks

  • Arc elasticity formula and a table: P1, P2, Q1, Q2, % changes, E, and PES or PED for each of the four markets
  • Interpretation: do the numbers match what the determinants of elasticity predict (necessity, substitutes, time period)?
  • Long run: how elasticity did, could or will change. Use the P3 (Q4 2021) data and relevant literature

Task 5: Conclusion (Islom) - 1 page, 10 marks

  • How the four markets' changes affected the economy and welfare (links across the markets, not four repeats)
  • Were the actions of the government and other institutions efficient? Judge them against the data
  • The two biggest limitations of the data and how they affect the conclusions
